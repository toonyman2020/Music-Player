Catti Cango & Mossquasa: The Champion of Rome
Final Story Summary

In a whimsical yet majestic animal-ruled version of Rome, Catti Cango — a chaotic orange tabby cat with a loaf body and wild spirit — arrives in dramatic fashion, parachuting from a chopper into the city. His goal: to challenge the undefeated hero of the arena, the legendary mouse gladiator Mossquasa, known for his yellow cloak, fierce speed, and beloved reputation as a protector of the people.

Catti, driven by pride, thrill, and a need to prove himself, challenges Mossquasa again and again in the arena. With every defeat, he grows more frustrated — but also more skilled. Mossquasa doesn’t just beat him; he teaches him. Quietly, respectfully, their rivalry deepens into something more profound. Catti trains harder, learns faster, and eventually adapts to the mouse's quick moves, building the reflexes and precision needed to finally match him. Mossquasa, small but mighty, becomes more than a rival — he becomes Catti’s greatest teacher and, unknowingly, his best friend.

By their fourth battle, the tension is thick. The crowd cheers for Mossquasa as always, but Catti — though stronger than ever — is unraveling inside. His obsession blinds him. In a flash of emotion, Catti swings a real blade, convinced Mossquasa will dodge it like every time before. But Mossquasa was mid-leap, committed to a finishing move of his own — and this time, he can’t escape. The blade lands.

Time freezes. The crowd gasps. Mossquasa falls.

Catti is horrified. He kneels beside his fallen rival, realizing too late that what he broke wasn’t just a body — but the heart of the entire city. The arena turns on him with rage and heartbreak. Catti flees in shame.

Days later, Catti visits Mossquasa in the hospital. The mouse is barely hanging on, but he smiles. They speak honestly at last. Mossquasa forgives him and calls him his brother in battle. He tells Catti their fights were a gift — that through struggle, they brought out the best in each other. Mossquasa brings Catti to meet his wife and three children. Catti is overwhelmed. The mouse who shaped him into a true warrior had a full, quiet life beyond the arena.

Together, they return to the colosseum one final time. The crowd still boos Catti, but Mossquasa raises a paw and silences them. With the last of his strength, he says:

“He is not my enemy. He is my greatest rival… and my greatest friend. Forgive him.”

He turns to Catti, whispers,

“Go on. They’re yours now… you earned it.”
And then he dies.

As silence falls, Mossquasa’s wife and children grieve. Catti remains motionless, broken. As he approaches the family to return Mossquasa’s champion belt and offer a sack of gold, the wife looks at him with quiet fury and sorrow.

“Are you happy now, Catti?”
“You got what you wanted.”

The weight of her words cuts deeper than any sword. Catti says nothing. He kneels, embraces the children, and walks away, cloaked in Mossquasa’s yellow cape.

In the final scene, Catti stands alone at Mossquasa’s grave, surrounded by the people of Rome. Once a brash challenger, now a silent guardian of a legacy he never meant to inherit. He came for glory — but left with grief, humility, and a story that would never be forgotten.

For in chasing greatness… he lost the one who gave it to him.
